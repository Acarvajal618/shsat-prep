Grammar Guide

The short, one-off Revising/Editing questions (Part A) — not tied to a reading passage — sourced straight from Practice Tests A–G and Kaplan 2025 Practice Tests 1–3: 21 real, verbatim exam questions across 7 categories, no workbook filler. Each one shows the complete original paragraph or sentence (never a clipped fragment) — try to spot the issue yourself, then press the button to see it underlined with the fix and the reasoning.

How to use this page: don't try to memorize the rules first. Read a category's strategy box once, then go through its examples one at a time — read the clean text, take a real guess, then reveal the answer. The goal is to start recognizing the shape of each error type on sight, the same way you'd recognize a friend's face before you could describe their features in words. Guessing wrong and then seeing why is more useful than reading the answer first.
Looking for the longer passage-based Revising/Editing questions (transitions, reordering sentences, cutting off-topic sentences, best intro/conclusion, matching style)? Those live in the companion Revising/Editing Passage Guide.

1Vague Pronoun1 example

A pronoun ("she," "he," "it," "they") that could refer to more than one noun earlier in the sentence/paragraph, so the reader can't tell who or what is meant.

How to solve it:
  • Find the pronoun, then look back for every noun it could possibly point to.
  • If there's more than one candidate (especially two people of the same gender), it's vague.
  • Fix: name the specific person/thing instead of using the pronoun.
Exam A — Standalone (AR48)
(1) Eliza and Brianna have been singing in their school chorus since they were in fourth grade. (2) The girls always sing a duet at the school talent show, and they take turns singing the national anthem before school sporting events. (3) Outside of school, she also sings in a choir made up of young and old members of her community. (4) Both girls hope that they will be able to continue singing for many more years.
Which sentence of the paragraph should be revised to correct a vague pronoun?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(3) Outside of school, she also sings in a choir made up of young and old members of her community.
Why: Two girls, Eliza and Brianna, were just introduced. "She" could mean either one — the reader has no way to know which. Fix: name her directly (e.g., "Eliza also sings...").
2Agreement3 examples

A verb, pronoun, or tense that doesn't match its subject/antecedent in number or time: a singular subject with a plural verb, a plural pronoun pointing at a singular noun, or a verb tense that drifts away from the rest of the paragraph.

How to solve it:
  • For every underlined verb, find its actual subject (ignore the words in between) and ask "singular or plural?"
  • For every underlined pronoun, find its antecedent the same way.
  • For tense, ask whether the paragraph is telling the story in one consistent timeframe — a single verb that jumps tense sticks out once you're looking for it.
Exam A — Standalone (AR49), "pair of revisions" format
(1) Yalina, Michael, and Malcolm love making pancakes with their granddad on Saturday mornings. (2) Yalina's job is to open the box and pour the pancake mix into a bowl, slowly adding water, eggs, melted butter, and blueberries. (3) Michael uses a wooden spoon to vigorously stir the mixture until it is smooth, and Malcolm helps Granddad carefully pour the batter onto a griddle one-fourth cup at a time. (4) Granddad turns each pancake when they start to bubble, while all three siblings get the table ready for a sweet delicious breakfast.
Which pair of revisions is needed to correct the errors in the paragraph?
  • A. Sentence 1: Delete the comma after Yalina, AND change their to her.
  • B. Sentence 2: Change is to are, AND delete the comma after bowl.
  • C. Sentence 3: Change it is to they are, AND delete the comma after smooth.
  • D. Sentence 4: Change they start to it starts, AND insert a comma after sweet.
(4) Granddad turns each pancake when they start to bubble... → when it starts to bubble, while all three siblings get the table ready for a sweet, delicious breakfast.
Why: The antecedent is "each pancake" — singular — so it needs "it starts," not "they start." Separately, "sweet" and "delicious" are two independent adjectives both describing "breakfast," so they need a comma between them. (Sentences 1–3's underlines are all already correct — that's what makes this format tricky.)
Exam G — Standalone (GR47)
(1) Both Salvadoran and Mexican tamales are delicious staples of Mesoamerican cuisine, but they differ in preparation, wrapping, and texture. (2) Salvadoran tamales are often prepared by pre-cooking the masa, a ground corn dough, into a thick, smooth roux before steaming it, resulting in a silky, custard-like texture that is much softer and wetter than that of typical Mexican tamales. (3) The preparation of Mexican tamales involves a fat, and broth, which is mixed into the masa before steaming. (4) Mexican tamales are traditionally wrapped in dried corn husks; Salvadoran tamales, however, are wrapped in banana leaves, which give them an earthy flavor and tint them slightly green.
Which pair of revisions need to be made in this paragraph?
  • A. Sentence 1: Delete the comma after cuisine AND change they differ to it differs.
  • B. Sentence 2: Delete the comma after dough AND change is to are.
  • C. Sentence 3: Delete the comma after fat AND change is to are.
  • D. Sentence 4: Delete the semicolon after husks AND change tint to tints.
(3) The preparation of Mexican tamales involves a fat, and broth, which is mixed into the masa before steaming. → a fat and broth, which are mixed into the masa before steaming.
Why: The subject of "is mixed" is "a fat...and broth" — a compound (two-part) subject, which is always plural, so it needs "are mixed." The stray comma after "fat" also gets deleted since "a fat and broth" is a simple pair, not a list.
Kaplan 1 — Standalone (KM1_Q05)
(1) With its dramatic canyons and gushing geysers in sections of Wyoming, Idaho, and Montana, Yellowstone National Park offers unique hydrothermal and geologic wonders. (2) While many tourists visited the park each year, they are a permanent home for thousands of animals, including bears, elk, and wolves. (3) During the spring, Yellowstone becomes a dynamic landscape, prompting nature lovers to flock to it to see the rushing rivers and flourishing plant and animal life. (4) Hundreds of animal species, including nearly 300 species of birds, 16 species of fish, and 67 species of mammals, have been observed making the park their home.
How should the paragraph be revised?
  • A. Sentence 1: Change its to their, AND change offers to offered.
  • B. Sentence 2: Change visited to visit, AND change they are to it is.
  • C. Sentence 3: Change becomes to became, AND change it to them.
  • D. Sentence 4: Change have been to had been, AND change their to its.
(2) While many tourists visited the park each year, they are a permanent home... → While many tourists visit the park each year, it is a permanent home for thousands of animals...
Why: Two separate agreement slips in one sentence: the rest of the paragraph is present tense ("offers," "becomes"), so "visited" needs to be "visit"; and "they" has no plural antecedent — it should be "it," referring back to "the park."
3Sentence Construction4 examples

Fragments (missing a real main verb), dangling modifiers (an opening phrase that describes something other than the subject right after the comma), and run-ons — the question usually just asks you to spot which sentence has the problem, not how to fix it.

How to solve it:
  • For every sentence, find the subject and find a verb that actually goes with it (not a participle like "-ing" pretending to be the verb).
  • If a sentence opens with an "-ing" or "-ed" phrase followed by a comma, check that the very next noun is the thing actually doing that action.
  • If it isn't, that's a dangling modifier.
Exam D — Standalone (DR13)
(1) Walking dogs, cleaning kennels, hand-feeding newborn kittens, and supporting the pet-adoption process, the animal shelter is looking for volunteers to help with a variety of tasks. (2) Working at the animal shelter is a great way for young people, especially those who aspire to care for and protect animals, to gain valuable work experience. (3) In addition to hands-on training with animal care, volunteers will learn important job skills, such as punctuality, responsibility, and personal initiative. (4) Caring for animals can also help volunteers develop empathy, which is the awareness and understanding of the feelings of others.
Which sentence contains an error in its construction and should be revised?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(1) Walking dogs, cleaning kennels, hand-feeding newborn kittens, and supporting the pet-adoption process, the animal shelter is looking for volunteers...
Why: It's not the shelter that walks dogs and feeds kittens — it's the volunteers. The opening phrase dangles because the noun right after the comma ("the animal shelter") isn't who's actually doing those actions.
Exam G — Standalone (GR49)
(1) On the morning of February 10, 2022, the world was captivated as Nathan Chen took to the ice at the Winter Olympics in Beijing, China. (2) Executing five flawless quadruple jumps during his "Rocket Man" free skate, the stadium erupted in applause, and his gold medal was secured. (3) Longtime figure skating fans were particularly moved because he was the first U.S. men's skater to win gold since Evan Lysacek in 2010. (4) Another American skater, Dick Button, won the first gold medal for the U.S. men's figure skating in 1948 by adding jumps to his program that no one else had attempted in international competition.
Which sentence in the paragraph contains an error in construction? Move the answer to the box. There is only one error in construction.

"Move the answer to the box" format — drag one of these into the box:

  • Sentence 1
  • Sentence 2
  • Sentence 3
  • Sentence 4
Correct: Sentence 2 — Executing five flawless quadruple jumps during his "Rocket Man" free skate, the stadium erupted in applause, and his gold medal was secured.
Why: The stadium didn't execute the jumps — Nathan Chen did. Same dangling-modifier pattern as DR13: check who's really doing the "-ing" action right after the comma.
Kaplan 3 — Standalone (KM3_Q01)
(1) John Updike wrote many novels, including the Pulitzer Prize-winning Rabbit Is Rich. (2) This famous American author studied at Harvard and Oxford before beginning his career in literature and eventually earning more than one Pulitzer Prize. (3) His novels exploring contemporary human relationships in the United States. (4) In addition to creating novels, Updike wrote poems and collections of short stories.
Which sentence contains an error in its construction and should be revised?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(3) His novels exploring contemporary human relationships in the United States.
Why: This is a fragment, not a complete sentence — "exploring" is a participle, not a real verb. It needs something like "explore" or "explored" to actually make the subject "his novels" do something.
Kaplan 2 — Standalone (KM2_Q02)
(1) After graduating from college, the next five years of a software engineer's life are spent working toward a graduate degree in computer science. (2) The coursework revolves around programming, which is the preparation of instructions that tell the computer which operations it needs to perform. (3) Students learn formulas as well as programs that translate complex computer languages. (4) With new advancements every day, the computer science field requires professionals to constantly learn about applying the latest technology.
Which sentence contains an error in its construction and should be revised?
  • E. sentence 1
  • F. sentence 2
  • G. sentence 3
  • H. sentence 4
(1) After graduating from college, the next five years of a software engineer's life are spent working toward a graduate degree...
Why: It's the software engineer who graduates from college, not "the next five years." Another dangling opening modifier — the subject right after the comma should be the one doing the graduating.
4Combining Sentences4 examples

Two short, related sentences need to become one sentence that makes the relationship between the ideas (cause/effect, contrast, extra detail) clear — not just stapled together with "and."

How to solve it:
  • Before looking at the answers, decide in your own words how the two ideas relate: cause/effect? contrast? just extra description of the same noun?
  • Pick the choice whose connecting word (because, although, which, but) matches that relationship.
  • Wrong answers usually connect the ideas with the wrong logic, even though the grammar is fine.
Exam B — Standalone (BR07)
(1) Flyby missions near Jupiter have been happening since 1973. (2) Flyby missions allow scientists to collect data about Jupiter and its moons.
What is the best way to combine the sentences to clarify the relationship between the ideas?
  • A. While flyby missions near Jupiter have been happening since 1973, scientists collect data about the planet and its moons.
  • B. Although there have been flyby missions near Jupiter since 1973, they have allowed scientists to collect data about the planet and its moons.
  • C. Flyby missions near Jupiter, which allow scientists to collect data about the planet and its moons, have been happening since 1973.
  • D. Flyby missions have been happening near Jupiter, but scientists have been collecting data about the planet and its moons since 1973.
"Flyby missions near Jupiter, which allow scientists to collect data about the planet and its moons, have been happening since 1973."
Why: Sentence 2 is just extra description of "flyby missions," so it folds into sentence 1 as a "which" clause rather than being tacked on with "and" or turned into a false cause/contrast.
Exam C — Standalone (CR49)
(1) Jordan rehearsed his role for weeks. (2) He delivered his lines with ease. (3) His performance was met with a burst of applause from the audience.
What is the best way to combine the sentences to clarify the relationship between ideas?
  • A. Though Jordan had rehearsed his role for weeks, when he delivered his lines with ease, his performance was met with a burst of applause from the audience.
  • B. Jordan had rehearsed his role for weeks, yet he delivered his lines with ease, so his performance was met with a burst of applause from the audience.
  • C. Because Jordan had rehearsed his role for weeks, he delivered his lines with ease, and his performance was met with a burst of applause from the audience.
  • D. Jordan had rehearsed his role for weeks, so he delivered his lines with ease, but his performance was met with a burst of applause from the audience.
"Because Jordan had rehearsed his role for weeks, he delivered his lines with ease, and his performance was met with a burst of applause from the audience."
Why: All three ideas are a cause-and-effect chain (rehearsal → ease → applause). "Because" correctly marks the cause; "and" just adds the final result. Wrong choices use "yet," "so...but" — words that imply contrast or a problem, which isn't what's happening here.
Exam D — Standalone (DR16)
(1) Scientists now believe that Jupiter may have as many as seventy-nine moons. (2) One of Jupiter's moons is named Io. (3) Io has the greatest number of active volcanoes in the solar system.
What is the best way to combine the sentences?
  • A. Io, which is one of Jupiter's moons, has the greatest number of active volcanoes in the solar system, and scientists now believe that Jupiter may have as many as seventy-nine moons.
  • B. Scientists now believe that Jupiter may have as many as seventy-nine moons, and one of them is named Io, which has the greatest number of active volcanoes in the solar system.
  • C. Scientists now believe that Jupiter may have as many as seventy-nine moons, including one named Io, which has the greatest number of active volcanoes in the solar system.
  • D. Io, a moon with the greatest number of active volcanoes in the solar system, is one of Jupiter's moons, and scientists now believe that Jupiter may have as many as seventy-nine moons.
"Scientists now believe that Jupiter may have as many as seventy-nine moons, including one named Io, which has the greatest number of active volcanoes in the solar system."
Why: Sentence 1 is the main idea; sentences 2–3 are both just further detail about one specific moon, so "including...which" nests them both as description rather than giving them equal weight with "and."
Exam F — Standalone (FR49)
(1) NASA's Artemis program plans to establish a base camp on the Moon. (2) The base camp established by NASA's Artemis program will serve as a critical staging point for future missions to Mars.
What is the best way to combine the sentences to clarify the relationship between the ideas?
  • A. NASA's Artemis program plans to establish a base camp on the Moon, but this lunar presence will serve as a critical staging point for future missions to Mars.
  • B. NASA's Artemis program plans to establish a base camp on the Moon; afterwards, this lunar presence will serve as a critical staging point for future missions to Mars.
  • C. Intending to serve as a critical staging point for future missions to Mars, the Moon will be used to establish a base camp by NASA's Artemis program.
  • D. Although NASA's Artemis program plans to establish a base camp on the Moon, this lunar presence will serve as a critical staging point for future missions to Mars.
"NASA's Artemis program plans to establish a base camp on the Moon; afterwards, this lunar presence will serve as a critical staging point for future missions to Mars."
Why: This is a sequence in time (base camp first, staging point after), not a contrast — "afterwards" correctly marks that order. "Although/but" choices would wrongly imply the two ideas conflict with each other.
5Punctuation5 examples

Missing, extra, or wrong punctuation — mainly comma splices (two complete sentences joined only by a comma, which needs a semicolon or period instead), and missing commas between two adjectives that each independently describe the same noun.

How to solve it:
  • Comma splice check: cover everything before the comma — is what's left a complete sentence on its own? Now cover everything after — is THAT a complete sentence too?
  • If both halves stand alone, a comma alone can't join them; it needs a semicolon or period.
  • Two-adjective check: if you could say "and" between the two describing words and it still makes sense (swift AND nimble), they need a comma between them.
Exam B — Standalone (BR06)
In September 2016 the National Museum of African American History and Culture opened as part of the Smithsonian Institution, the museum is already the Smithsonian's third most popular site. Experts say that they expect this newest Smithsonian facility to welcome nearly 4 million visitors a year. The museum features more than 30,000 objects, including Muhammad Ali's boxing gloves and a dress sewn by Rosa Parks. A commemorative copy of the Emancipation Proclamation, written in 1863 during the presidency of Abraham Lincoln, is also on display at the museum.
Which part of the paragraph contains an error in sentence structure? Select the one error.
  • A. Institution, the
  • B. year. The
  • C. objects, including
  • D. Proclamation, written
"...opened as part of the Smithsonian Institution, the museum is already..." → "...Smithsonian Institution; the museum is already..."
Why: "The museum opened..." and "the museum is already..." are both complete sentences on their own — a plain comma can't join two complete sentences, so it needs a semicolon. (The other three underlined spots are correctly punctuated — they're just decoys.)
Kaplan 1 — Standalone (KM1_Q01)
One of the most predictable problems that New England gardeners encounter is rocky soil, another issue that plagues these gardeners is the destruction of cultivated plants by local wildlife.
Which edit should be made to correct this sentence?
  • A. Insert a comma after problems.
  • B. Insert a comma after encounter.
  • C. Delete the comma after soil.
  • D. Replace the comma after soil with a semicolon.
"...is rocky soil, another issue that plagues..." → "...is rocky soil; another issue that plagues these gardeners is the destruction of cultivated plants by local wildlife."
Why: Same comma-splice pattern as BR06 — "...is rocky soil" and "another issue...is the destruction..." are each a full sentence, so the comma needs to become a semicolon.
Exam G — Standalone (GR50)
The dedicated skilled engineer Ellen Ochoa became the first Hispanic American woman to go to space when she participated in a nine-day NASA mission focused on studying the Sun and its interaction with Earth's atmosphere in 1993.
Which edit should be made to correct the sentence?
  • A. Insert a comma after dedicated.
  • B. Insert a comma after Hispanic.
  • C. Insert a comma after nine-day.
  • D. Insert a comma after Sun.
"The dedicated skilled engineer Ellen Ochoa became..." → "The dedicated, skilled engineer Ellen Ochoa became..."
Why: "Dedicated" and "skilled" are two separate, equal adjectives both describing "engineer" ("dedicated AND skilled engineer" works), so they need a comma between them.
Kaplan 3 — Standalone (KM3_Q06)
The swift nimble Florence Griffith Joyner set a world record in 1988 when she beat the old record for the 100-meter sprint by 0.27 seconds and improved her previous best by more than half a second.
Which edit should be made to correct this sentence?
  • E. Insert a comma after swift.
  • F. Insert a comma after seconds.
  • G. Insert a comma after previous.
  • H. Insert a comma after best.
"The swift nimble Florence Griffith Joyner..." → "The swift, nimble Florence Griffith Joyner..."
Why: Identical pattern to GR50 — two independent adjectives ("swift" and "nimble") describing the same person need a comma between them.
Kaplan 3 — Standalone (KM3_Q03)
Point Pleasant needed increased revenue, from tourism, and consequently the town built a new park, a large hotel, and a fine restaurant in the main square.
How should the sentence be revised?
  • A. Insert a comma after Point Pleasant, AND delete the comma after hotel.
  • B. Delete the comma after revenue, AND insert a comma after consequently.
  • C. Insert a comma after town, AND delete the comma after park.
  • D. Insert a comma after restaurant, AND insert a comma after main.
"Point Pleasant needed increased revenue, from tourism, and consequently the town built..." → "Point Pleasant needed increased revenue from tourism, and, consequently, the town built..."
Why: "Revenue from tourism" is one tight phrase that shouldn't be split by a comma; "consequently" is an inserted transition word that needs its own commas on both sides, not a bare "and consequently."
6Precise Language4 examples

A vague, wordy, or generic phrase that could be replaced with a more specific, concrete one that still fits the facts already given in the passage.

How to solve it:
  • Reread the sentence/passage for concrete details (numbers, names, specific nouns) near the vague phrase.
  • The correct answer always uses details that were already established — it never invents new facts.
  • Wrong answers are usually either still vague, or precise-sounding but about the wrong detail/invented information.
Exam D — Standalone (DR14)
In the Whispering Gallery of Grand Central Terminal, people whispering a message in one corner can be clearly heard in the opposite corner, regardless of surrounding noise, because the tiled archways send the sound through the environment.
Which revision to the words "send the sound through the environment" uses the most precise language? Drag the correct phrase into the box.

Drag-the-phrase format — drag one of these into the box:

  • guide the sound waves in a specific direction
  • focus the sound waves to travel through the space
  • direct the sound waves to move along the curves of the arches
  • transfer the sound waves to a variety of different locations
"...because the tiled archways send the sound through the environment." → "...because the tiled archways direct the sound waves to move along the curves of the arches."
Why: "Send...through the environment" is vague — it doesn't say how. The fix names the actual mechanism already implied by "tiled archways": sound waves following the curve of the arch.
Exam F — Standalone (FR47)
Lucia explored the flea market looking for some cheap things for her kitchen. After searching through a bin of mismatched ceramics, she finally discovered a set of hand-carved cherry wood bowls that possessed exactly the rustic charm she had imagined. With a few polished silver spoons tucked into her bag alongside them, she headed home, satisfied that she had found the perfect additions to her kitchen without exceeding her budget.
What is the most precise revision for the words "some cheap things for her kitchen"?
  • A. some inexpensive things for her kitchen
  • B. some low-cost kitchen items
  • C. two types of kitchen tools
  • D. affordable salad bowls and serving spoons
"Lucia explored the flea market looking for some cheap things for her kitchen." → "...looking for affordable salad bowls and serving spoons."
Why: The rest of the paragraph already tells us exactly what she bought — bowls and spoons. The precise revision uses those specific items instead of "some cheap things." ("Inexpensive"/"low-cost" choices are just a synonym swap — still vague about WHAT she bought.)
Exam E — Standalone (ER50)
The Brooklyn Bridge was such a big distance across the water and took a really long time to finish, so many people doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
Which revision of the sentence uses the most precise language?
  • A. The Brooklyn Bridge involved a significant gap across the water and lasted for many years, so many people doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
  • B. The Brooklyn Bridge required a one-mile span over the East River and fourteen years of construction, so many people doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
  • C. The Brooklyn Bridge was an immense length over the river and its construction continued for a lengthy duration, so many people doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
  • D. The Brooklyn Bridge featured a wide reach between landmasses and took an extended amount of time, so many people doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
"The Brooklyn Bridge was such a big distance across the water and took a really long time to finish..." → "The Brooklyn Bridge required a one-mile span over the East River and fourteen years of construction..."
Why: "Big distance" and "really long time" are vague; the best choice swaps in the actual measurable facts (one mile, fourteen years, East River) rather than just rephrasing the vagueness with fancier synonyms like "immense length" or "lengthy duration."
Kaplan 3 — Standalone (KM3_Q02)
During the intense exchange between the basketball coaches and the referees about a pivotal decision that could change the outcome of the playoff game, the sports journalist calmly and clearly explained to all of the people who were viewing channel KXTV on their televisions what was happening.
Which revision uses the most precise language for the underlined words?
  • E. told the people about the events
  • F. reported on the disagreement for KXTV
  • G. explained the dialogue for the people at home watching KXTV
  • H. discussed the issue on live television
"...the sports journalist calmly and clearly explained to all of the people who were viewing channel KXTV on their televisions what was happening." → "...the sports journalist calmly and clearly reported on the disagreement for KXTV."
Why: "All of the people who were viewing channel KXTV on their televisions" is a wordy way of just saying "for KXTV," and "what was happening" is replaced with the specific event already named earlier in the sentence: "the disagreement."
7Commonly Confused Words & Word Forms0 examples yet

Small, specific mix-ups that show up on these tests again and again: possessive vs. contraction ("its" vs. "it's"), "who" vs. "that" for people, irregular past participles ("rang" vs. "rung"), and comparative vs. superlative ("better" vs. "best").

How to solve it: These aren't about logic — they're about recognizing five or six specific, recurring mix-ups on sight.
  • its/it's: "it's" always means "it is" — if "it is" doesn't fit, it should be "its."
  • who/that: "who" for people, "that" for things.
  • Comparative vs. superlative: exactly 2 things being compared → "-er"/"more" (better, more); 3+ things → "-est"/"most" (best, most).
  • Irregular verbs: after "has/have/was/were," use the participle form (rung, swum, begun), not the simple past (rang, swam, began).
