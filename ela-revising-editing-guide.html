Grammar Guide

The short, one-off Revising/Editing questions (Part A) — not tied to a reading passage — grouped into 8 skills, 3 examples each. Every question on this page is an original, freshly written question, not a real one. Each card notes what it's modeled on: a real SHSAT standalone question ("Same skill as Exam X..."), a real Kaplan-workbook fodder question ("Same skill as Fodder — GRFnnn..."), or nothing at all ("Original example") when neither source had a match. Either way, the real questions stay unseen here and ready for real practice — for a much bigger pool of real, answerable fodder questions to actually work through, see the Grammar Question Bank.

How to use this page: don't try to memorize the rules first. Read a category's strategy box once, then go through its examples one at a time — read the clean text, take a real guess, then reveal the answer. The goal is to start recognizing the shape of each error type on sight, the same way you'd recognize a friend's face before you could describe their features in words.
Looking for the longer passage-based Revising/Editing questions (transitions, reordering sentences, cutting off-topic sentences, best intro/conclusion, matching style)? Those live in the companion Revising/Editing Passage Guide.

1Vague Pronoun3 examples — 2 from real questions, 1 original

A pronoun ("she," "he," "it," "they") that could refer to more than one noun earlier in the sentence/paragraph, so the reader can't tell who or what is meant.

How to solve it:
  • Find the pronoun, then look back for every noun it could possibly point to.
  • If there's more than one candidate (especially two people of the same gender), it's vague.
  • Fix: name the specific person/thing instead of using the pronoun.
Same skill as Exam A — Standalone (AR48) — not the real question
(1) Marcus and Devon have played on the same soccer team since elementary school. (2) The boys practice drills together every weekend and often carpool to tournaments. (3) After practice, he usually stays late to work on free kicks with the coach. (4) Both boys hope to make the varsity team next year.
Which sentence of the paragraph should be revised to correct a vague pronoun?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(3) After practice, he usually stays late to work on free kicks with the coach.
Why: Marcus and Devon were both just introduced. "He" could mean either one — the reader has no way to know which. Fix: name him directly (e.g., "After practice, Devon usually stays late...").
Same skill as Exam G — Standalone (GR48) — not the real question
(1) Priya and Keisha have been playing violin since they were in second grade. (2) Her mother studied music theory in college and now teaches private lessons on weekends. (3) The girls always rehearse duets together before recitals, and they take turns playing the melody and harmony parts. (4) Both girls plan to audition for the youth orchestra next spring.
Which sentence in the paragraph contains an error in construction? There is only one error in construction.
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(2) Her mother studied music theory in college and now teaches private lessons on weekends.
Why: "Her" could refer to either Priya's mother or Keisha's mother — there's no way to tell which. Same pattern as the first example: a pronoun shows up right after two people were named, with no single clear antecedent.
Original example — not based on a specific real exam question
The museum curator placed the ancient vase on a shelf above the ornate mirror, worried that it might be damaged during the renovation.
Which revision best fixes the vague pronoun in this sentence?
  • A. ...worried that it might be damaged during the renovation. (no change)
  • B. ...worried that the vase might be damaged during the renovation.
  • C. ...worried that the shelf might be damaged during the renovation.
  • D. ...worried that the renovation might be damaged.
"...worried that it might be damaged during the renovation." → "...worried that the vase might be damaged during the renovation."
Why: Vague pronouns aren't just about people — "it" here could mean the vase OR the mirror, since both are singular objects named right before it. Naming the specific object removes the ambiguity.
2Agreement3 examples

A verb, pronoun, or tense that doesn't match its subject/antecedent in number or time: a singular subject with a plural verb, a plural pronoun pointing at a singular noun, or a verb tense that drifts away from the rest of the paragraph.

How to solve it:
  • For every underlined verb, find its actual subject (ignore the words in between) and ask "singular or plural?"
  • For every underlined pronoun, find its antecedent the same way.
  • For tense, ask whether the paragraph is telling the story in one consistent timeframe — a single verb that jumps tense sticks out once you're looking for it.
Same skill as Exam A — Standalone (AR49), "pair of revisions" format — not the real question
(1) Noah, Priya, and Deja love blending smoothies with their grandmother on Sunday afternoons. (2) Priya's job is to wash the fruit and cut it into small pieces, carefully adding yogurt and ice to the blender. (3) Deja presses the blender button until the mixture is smooth, and Noah helps Grandma pour the finished smoothie into cups. (4) Grandma blends each smoothie until they turn smooth, while all three grandchildren set the table for a cold refreshing treat.
Which pair of revisions is needed to correct the errors in the paragraph?
  • A. Sentence 1: Delete the comma after Noah, AND change their to her.
  • B. Sentence 2: Change is to are, AND delete the comma after pieces.
  • C. Sentence 3: Change is to are, AND delete the comma after smooth.
  • D. Sentence 4: Change they turn to it turns, AND insert a comma after cold.
(4) Grandma blends each smoothie until they turn smooth... → until it turns smooth, while all three grandchildren set the table for a cold, refreshing treat.
Why: The antecedent is "each smoothie" — singular — so it needs "it turns," not "they turn." Separately, "cold" and "refreshing" are two independent adjectives both describing "treat," so they need a comma between them.
Same skill as Exam G — Standalone (GR47) — not the real question
(1) Both Neapolitan and Chicago-style pizzas are beloved dishes in American cuisine, but they differ in crust, toppings, and cooking method. (2) Neapolitan pizza is traditionally made by stretching the dough very thin before topping it with San Marzano tomatoes and fresh mozzarella, resulting in a light, chewy crust that bakes quickly in a wood-fired oven. (3) The preparation of Chicago-style pizza involves a layer of cheese, and sauce, which is added before the crust is baked in a deep pan. (4) Neapolitan pizzas are traditionally served unsliced; Chicago-style pizzas, however, are cut into squares, which give them a distinctive party-style presentation.
Which pair of revisions need to be made in this paragraph?
  • A. Sentence 1: Delete the comma after cuisine AND change they differ to it differs.
  • B. Sentence 2: Delete the comma after dough AND change is to are.
  • C. Sentence 3: Delete the comma after cheese AND change is to are.
  • D. Sentence 4: Delete the semicolon after unsliced AND change give to gives.
(3) The preparation of Chicago-style pizza involves a layer of cheese, and sauce, which is added before the crust is baked in a deep pan. → a layer of cheese and sauce, which are added before the crust is baked in a deep pan.
Why: The subject of "is added" is "a layer of cheese...and sauce" — a compound (two-part) subject, which is always plural, so it needs "are added." The stray comma after "cheese" also gets deleted since "a layer of cheese and sauce" is a simple pair, not a list.
Same skill as Exam F — Standalone (FR48) — not the real question
(1) Comets are icy bodies that produce glowing tails, known as comas, as they near the Sun. (2) Their intense heat causes so much gas release that the comet appears to glow brighter than nearby stars. (3) The largest comets can develop tails that stretch for millions of miles. (4) Because these comets can provide clues about the early solar system, scientists used powerful telescopes to study their composition and origin.
Which sentence in the paragraph contains an error in construction and should be revised? There is only one error in construction.
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(4) ...scientists used powerful telescopes to study their composition and origin. → scientists use powerful telescopes to study their composition and origin.
Why: The rest of the paragraph is present tense ("are," "causes," "can develop"), so "used" breaks that consistency — nothing in the sentence signals that this one action happened only in the past. It should be "use."
3Sentence Construction3 examples

Fragments (missing a real main verb), dangling modifiers (an opening phrase that describes something other than the subject right after the comma), misplaced modifiers (a descriptive phrase sitting next to the wrong noun), and run-ons — the question usually just asks you to spot which sentence has the problem, not how to fix it.

How to solve it:
  • For every sentence, find the subject and find a verb that actually goes with it (not a participle like "-ing" pretending to be the verb).
  • If a sentence opens with an "-ing" or "-ed" phrase followed by a comma, check that the very next noun is the thing actually doing that action — if it isn't, that's a dangling modifier.
  • For any descriptive phrase in the middle of a sentence (like "such as..."), check it sits right next to the specific thing it's describing — if it's next to the wrong noun, that's a misplaced modifier.
Same skill as Exam D — Standalone (DR13) — not the real question
(1) Sorting donations, organizing shelves, and greeting visitors, the community food pantry is looking for volunteers to help on weekends. (2) Working at the food pantry is a great way for students, especially those interested in social work, to gain hands-on experience. (3) In addition to packing food boxes, volunteers learn communication skills, such as patience, teamwork, and active listening. (4) Helping others can also build a volunteer's sense of purpose, which is the feeling that one's work matters.
Which sentence contains an error in its construction and should be revised?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(1) Sorting donations, organizing shelves, and greeting visitors, the community food pantry is looking for volunteers...
Why: It's not the pantry that sorts donations and greets visitors — it's the volunteers. The opening phrase dangles because the noun right after the comma ("the community food pantry") isn't who's actually doing those actions.
Same skill as Kaplan 3 — Standalone (KM3_Q01) — not the real question
(1) Maya Angelou wrote many acclaimed works, including the celebrated memoir I Know Why the Caged Bird Sings. (2) This influential American author studied dance and drama before beginning her career in writing and eventually earning numerous honorary degrees. (3) Her poems exploring themes of resilience and identity throughout American history. (4) In addition to writing memoirs, Angelou composed poetry and essays.
Which sentence contains an error in its construction and should be revised?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(3) Her poems exploring themes of resilience and identity throughout American history.
Why: This is a fragment, not a complete sentence — "exploring" is a participle, not a real verb. It needs something like "explore" or "explored" to actually make the subject "her poems" do something.
Same skill as Kaplan 1 — Standalone (KM1_Q06) — not the real question
(1) There is considerable evidence that early trade networks were greatly influenced by how well merchants could navigate difficult mountain terrain, such as the Silk Road traders crossing the Himalayas. (2) The development of pack animals allowed for much more efficient transport, which created new opportunities for long-distance commerce. (3) In addition to carrying goods, traders often exchanged ideas and technologies along these routes. (4) Greater connectivity and shared knowledge allowed civilizations along these routes to develop faster than was possible in more isolated regions.
Which sentence should be revised to correct an error in sentence structure?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(1) There is considerable evidence that early trade networks were greatly influenced by how well merchants could navigate difficult mountain terrain, such as the Silk Road traders crossing the Himalayas.
Why: "Such as the Silk Road traders crossing the Himalayas" is meant to give an example of "early trade networks," but as written it's placed right after "mountain terrain" — making it sound like the terrain is being exemplified, not the trade networks. A misplaced modifier needs to move next to the thing it actually describes.
4Combining Sentences3 examples

Two short, related sentences need to become one sentence that makes the relationship between the ideas (cause/effect, contrast, extra detail) clear — not just stapled together with "and."

How to solve it:
  • Before looking at the answers, decide in your own words how the two ideas relate: cause/effect? contrast? just extra description of the same noun?
  • Pick the choice whose connecting word (because, although, which, but) matches that relationship.
  • Wrong answers usually connect the ideas with the wrong logic, even though the grammar is fine.
Same skill as Exam C — Standalone (CR49) — not the real question
(1) Elena practiced her violin piece for months. (2) She performed the solo with confidence. (3) Her performance earned a standing ovation from the audience.
What is the best way to combine the sentences to clarify the relationship between ideas?
  • A. Though Elena had practiced her violin piece for months, when she performed the solo with confidence, her performance earned a standing ovation from the audience.
  • B. Elena had practiced her violin piece for months, yet she performed the solo with confidence, so her performance earned a standing ovation from the audience.
  • C. Because Elena had practiced her violin piece for months, she performed the solo with confidence, and her performance earned a standing ovation from the audience.
  • D. Elena had practiced her violin piece for months, so she performed the solo with confidence, but her performance earned a standing ovation from the audience.
"Because Elena had practiced her violin piece for months, she performed the solo with confidence, and her performance earned a standing ovation from the audience."
Why: All three ideas are a cause-and-effect chain (practice → confidence → ovation). "Because" correctly marks the cause; "and" just adds the final result. Wrong choices use "yet," "so...but" — words that imply contrast or a problem, which isn't what's happening here.
Same skill as Kaplan 1 — Standalone (KM1_Q03) — not the real question
(1) Young gymnasts need to dedicate years of intense training in order to compete at an elite level. (2) This training is impossible without the guidance and support of experienced coaches.
What is the best way to combine the sentences to clarify the relationship between the ideas?
  • A. Young gymnasts have years of intense training they need to dedicate in order to compete at an elite level; however, they also need the guidance of experienced coaches.
  • B. While young gymnasts have years of intense training they need to dedicate in order to compete at an elite level, they also need the guidance and support of experienced coaches.
  • C. The years of intense training that young gymnasts need to dedicate in order to compete at an elite level is impossible without the guidance and support of experienced coaches.
  • D. The years of intense training that young gymnasts have, along with the guidance of experienced coaches, is needed to compete at an elite level.
"The years of intense training that young gymnasts need to dedicate in order to compete at an elite level is impossible without the guidance and support of experienced coaches."
Why: Sentence 1 says gymnasts need to dedicate years of training; sentence 2 adds that this is only possible with coaches' support. Option C keeps both main ideas intact by directly linking them with "is impossible without" — the same relationship the original two sentences state. The other choices shift the emphasis (A, B make it sound like two loosely related facts) or restructure the idea in a way that changes what's being said (D).
Same skill as Exam F — Standalone (FR49) — not the real question
(1) The city's new transit authority plans to build a light-rail line downtown. (2) The light-rail line built by the transit authority will serve as a key connector for future suburban extensions.
What is the best way to combine the sentences to clarify the relationship between the ideas?
  • A. The city's new transit authority plans to build a light-rail line downtown, but this rail line will serve as a key connector for future suburban extensions.
  • B. The city's new transit authority plans to build a light-rail line downtown; afterwards, this rail line will serve as a key connector for future suburban extensions.
  • C. Intending to serve as a key connector for future suburban extensions, the downtown will be used to build a light-rail line by the transit authority.
  • D. Although the city's new transit authority plans to build a light-rail line downtown, this rail line will serve as a key connector for future suburban extensions.
"The city's new transit authority plans to build a light-rail line downtown; afterwards, this rail line will serve as a key connector for future suburban extensions."
Why: This is a sequence in time (light-rail line first, connector role after), not a contrast — "afterwards" correctly marks that order. "Although/but" choices would wrongly imply the two ideas conflict with each other.
5Punctuation3 examples

Missing, extra, or wrong punctuation — mainly comma splices (two complete sentences joined only by a comma, which needs a semicolon or period instead), and missing commas between two adjectives that each independently describe the same noun.

How to solve it:
  • Comma splice check: cover everything before the comma — is what's left a complete sentence on its own? Now cover everything after — is THAT a complete sentence too?
  • If both halves stand alone, a comma alone can't join them; it needs a semicolon or period.
  • Two-adjective check: if you could say "and" between the two describing words and it still makes sense (swift AND nimble), they need a comma between them.
Same skill as Exam B — Standalone (BR06) — not the real question
In March 2015 the National Museum of Mathematics opened as part of a growing wave of hands-on science museums, the museum is already one of the city's most visited cultural sites. Experts say that they expect this growing museum to welcome over 100,000 visitors a year. The museum features more than 30 interactive exhibits, including a square-wheeled tricycle and a human-sized fractal maze. A rotating exhibit on probability, designed in 2019 by a team of mathematicians, is also on display at the museum.
Which part of the paragraph contains an error in sentence structure? Select the one error.
  • A. museums, the
  • B. year. The
  • C. exhibits, including
  • D. probability, designed
"...opened as part of a growing wave of hands-on science museums, the museum is already..." → "...hands-on science museums; the museum is already..."
Why: "The museum opened..." and "the museum is already..." are both complete sentences on their own — a plain comma can't join two complete sentences, so it needs a semicolon. (The other three underlined spots are correctly punctuated — they're just decoys.)
Same skill as Exam G — Standalone (GR50) — not the real question
The curious determined astronomer Dr. Lena Park became the first woman to lead the observatory's research team when she completed a six-month expedition focused on mapping distant galaxies in 2021.
Which edit should be made to correct the sentence?
  • A. Insert a comma after curious.
  • B. Insert a comma after woman.
  • C. Insert a comma after six-month.
  • D. Insert a comma after galaxies.
"The curious determined astronomer Dr. Lena Park became..." → "The curious, determined astronomer Dr. Lena Park became..."
Why: "Curious" and "determined" are two separate, equal adjectives both describing "astronomer" ("curious AND determined astronomer" works), so they need a comma between them.
Same skill as Kaplan 1 — Standalone (KM1_Q02) — not the real question
Many historians agree that Ada Lovelace, a pioneering mathematician is nearly as influential to computer science as she is to the history of mathematics.
Which edit should be made to correct this sentence?
  • A. Delete the comma after Lovelace.
  • B. Insert a comma after mathematician.
  • C. Insert a comma after influential.
  • D. Insert a comma after history.
"Many historians agree that Ada Lovelace, a pioneering mathematician is nearly as influential..." → "...Ada Lovelace, a pioneering mathematician, is nearly as influential..."
Why: "A pioneering mathematician" is nonessential extra information about Ada Lovelace, so it needs a comma on BOTH sides to be properly set off — the sentence only has the opening comma, not the closing one.
6Precise Language3 examples

A vague, wordy, or generic phrase that could be replaced with a more specific, concrete one that still fits the facts already given in the passage.

How to solve it:
  • Reread the sentence/passage for concrete details (numbers, names, specific nouns) near the vague phrase.
  • The correct answer always uses details that were already established — it never invents new facts.
  • Wrong answers are usually either still vague, or precise-sounding but about the wrong detail/invented information.
Same skill as Exam F — Standalone (FR47) — not the real question
Diego wandered the farmers market looking for some fresh things for his breakfast. After sampling a few bins of early-season berries, he finally found a crate of heirloom tomatoes that had exactly the ripeness he was hoping for. With a loaf of sourdough tucked under his arm, he headed home, pleased that he had found the perfect additions to his breakfast without spending too much.
What is the most precise revision for the underlined words?
  • A. some healthy things for his breakfast
  • B. some fresh market items
  • C. two types of produce
  • D. ripe tomatoes and a loaf of sourdough
"Diego wandered the farmers market looking for some fresh things for his breakfast." → "...looking for ripe tomatoes and a loaf of sourdough."
Why: The rest of the paragraph already tells us exactly what he bought — tomatoes and sourdough. The precise revision uses those specific items instead of "some fresh things."
Same skill as Exam E — Standalone (ER50) — not the real question
The Golden Gate Bridge was such a big distance across the water and took a really long time to finish, so many engineers doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
Which revision of the sentence uses the most precise language?
  • A. The Golden Gate Bridge involved a significant gap across the water and lasted for many years, so many engineers doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
  • B. The Golden Gate Bridge required a 1.7-mile span across the strait and four years of construction, so many engineers doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
  • C. The Golden Gate Bridge was an immense length over the strait and its construction continued for a lengthy duration, so many engineers doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
  • D. The Golden Gate Bridge featured a wide reach between shorelines and took an extended amount of time, so many engineers doubted that the bridge would ever be completed.
"The Golden Gate Bridge was such a big distance across the water and took a really long time to finish..." → "The Golden Gate Bridge required a 1.7-mile span across the strait and four years of construction..."
Why: "Big distance" and "really long time" are vague; the best choice swaps in the actual measurable facts (1.7 miles, four years, the strait) rather than just rephrasing the vagueness with fancier synonyms.
Same skill as Kaplan 3 — Standalone (KM3_Q02) — not the real question
During the heated debate between the city council members and the residents about a controversial zoning proposal that could change the character of the neighborhood, the local reporter calmly and clearly explained to all of the people who were reading the morning paper what was happening.
Which revision uses the most precise language for the underlined words?
  • E. told the people about the events
  • F. reported on the dispute for the morning paper
  • G. explained the debate for the people reading the paper
  • H. discussed the issue in print
"...the local reporter calmly and clearly explained to all of the people who were reading the morning paper what was happening." → "...the local reporter calmly and clearly reported on the dispute for the morning paper."
Why: "All of the people who were reading the morning paper" is a wordy way of just saying "for the morning paper," and "what was happening" is replaced with the specific event already named earlier in the sentence: "the dispute."
7Wordiness3 examples — 1 from a real question, 2 original

A sentence or clause that repeats information already stated elsewhere in the same sentence, using more words than needed to say the same thing.

How to solve it:
  • For each descriptive clause or phrase, ask: does this tell me something I don't already know from the rest of the sentence?
  • If a clause just restates a word that's already there in different words (e.g., "the professor, who teaches college students" — professors already teach college students), it's redundant.
  • Watch for redundant word pairs too, like "each and every" or "every single one" — both halves are doing the exact same job.
  • The fix is almost always to delete the redundant part, not to rephrase it.
Same skill as Kaplan 2 — Standalone (KM2_Q01) — not the real question
(1) A skilled surgeon, the doctor, who treats patients, is known throughout the hospital for steady hands and careful technique. (2) In the operating room, she explains each step of the procedure to her residents, who are training to become surgeons themselves. (3) Surgeons use specialized instruments that allow for precise, minimally invasive procedures. (4) Studying surgery requires years of hands-on practice in addition to classroom learning.
Which sentence should be revised to correct a wordiness issue?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(1) A skilled surgeon, the doctor, who treats patients, is known throughout the hospital for steady hands and careful technique.
Why: "Who treats patients" is redundant — the sentence already says "doctor" and "surgeon," both of which already mean someone who treats patients. The clause doesn't add any new information, it just restates what's already known.
Original example — not based on a specific real exam question
(1) The committee reviewed each and every application before making a final decision. (2) Applicants were asked to submit a résumé, a cover letter, and two references. (3) The strongest candidates were invited back for a second round of interviews. (4) The committee announced its decision by email the following week.
Which sentence should be revised to correct a wordiness issue?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(1) The committee reviewed each and every application before making a final decision. → The committee reviewed every application before making a final decision.
Why: "Each" and "every" mean the same thing here — pairing them doesn't add emphasis, it just says the same word twice. One or the other is enough.
Original example — not based on a specific real exam question
(1) The research team spent months collecting data before they began their final analysis. (2) Every single one of the participants completed the entire survey without skipping any questions. (3) The results revealed a clear connection between sleep quality and memory performance. (4) Researchers plan to publish their findings in a peer-reviewed journal next year.
Which sentence should be revised to correct a wordiness issue?
  • A. sentence 1
  • B. sentence 2
  • C. sentence 3
  • D. sentence 4
(2) Every single one of the participants completed the entire survey... → All of the participants completed the entire survey...
Why: "Every," "single," and "one" are all doing the same job of emphasizing totality — stacking all three doesn't add meaning, it just repeats it. "All of the participants" or "Every participant" says the exact same thing in fewer words.
8Commonly Confused Words & Word Forms3 examples

Small, specific mix-ups that show up on these tests again and again: possessive vs. contraction ("its" vs. "it's"), "who" vs. "that" for people, irregular past participles ("rang" vs. "rung"), and comparative vs. superlative ("better" vs. "best").

How to solve it: These aren't about logic — they're about recognizing five or six specific, recurring mix-ups on sight.
  • its/it's: "it's" always means "it is" — if "it is" doesn't fit, it should be "its."
  • who/that: "who" for people, "that" for things.
  • Comparative vs. superlative: exactly 2 things being compared → "-er"/"more" (better, more); 3+ things → "-est"/"most" (best, most).
  • Irregular verbs: after "has/have/was/were," use the participle form (rung, swum, begun), not the simple past (rang, swam, began).
Same skill as Fodder — GRF027 — not the real question
Although the mural was painted decades ago, it's vibrant colors still catch the eye of every visitor who walks past the community center.
Which edit should be made to correct this sentence?
  • A. Change it's to its.
  • B. Insert a comma after mural.
  • C. Insert a comma after colors.
  • D. Change catch to catches.
"...it's vibrant colors still catch the eye..." → "...its vibrant colors still catch the eye..."
Why: "It's" always means "it is" — try the substitution: "it is vibrant colors" doesn't make sense, so this needs the possessive "its" (no apostrophe).
Same skill as Fodder — GRF053 — not the real question
The old warning bell, which had not been rang since the school's founding, was tested again before the new semester began.
Which edit should be made to correct this sentence?
  • A. Change had not been rang to had not been rung.
  • B. Insert a comma after bell.
  • C. Change was tested to is tested.
  • D. Insert a comma after again.
"...which had not been rang since the school's founding..." → "...which had not been rung since the school's founding..."
Why: After "had been" (passive voice), irregular verbs need their participle form — "rung," not the simple past "rang." You'd say "the bell rang," but "the bell had been rung."
Same skill as Fodder — GRF159 — not the real question
The volunteers that organized the fundraiser spent weeks contacting local businesses for donations.
Which edit should be made to correct this sentence?
  • A. Change that to who.
  • B. Insert a comma after fundraiser.
  • C. Change spent to spend.
  • D. Insert a comma after weeks.
"The volunteers that organized the fundraiser..." → "The volunteers who organized the fundraiser..."
Why: "Volunteers" are people — "who" should be used for people, while "that" is reserved for things, animals, or groups treated as a single unit.
