Revising & Editing Passage Guide

The longer Revising/Editing questions that are attached to a full reading passage (Part B) — where you read several numbered sentences of a paragraph and answer questions about the paragraph as a whole, not just one sentence. Sourced straight from Practice Tests A–G and Kaplan 2025 Practice Tests 1–3: 20 real, verbatim exam questions across 5 categories, no workbook filler. Each one shows the complete relevant paragraph (never a clipped fragment) — try to spot the issue yourself, then press the button to see the reasoning.

How to use this page: don't try to memorize the rules first. Read a category's strategy box once, then go through its examples one at a time — read the full paragraph, take a real guess, then reveal the answer. These questions are less about spotting one broken word and more about understanding how a whole paragraph is organized and why one sentence fits (or doesn't) where it sits.
Looking for the short, one-off Revising/Editing questions (vague pronouns, agreement, sentence construction, combining sentences, punctuation, precise language, confused words) that aren't tied to a passage? Those live in the companion Grammar Guide.

1Transitions4 examples

Choosing the right connecting word/phrase (however, meanwhile, as a result, for example...) to open a sentence, based on exactly how it relates logically to the sentence right before it.

How to solve it:
  • Read only the sentence before and the sentence in question.
  • Name the relationship in plain words first: a contrast, a cause/effect, a new example, a shift in time/place, or an extra matching point?
  • Match that to the transition category — however/but = contrast; therefore/as a result = effect; for example/indeed = example or reinforcement; meanwhile = same time, different place.
Exam A — "The Benefits of Indoor Plants" (AR44)
(4) The NASA Clean Air Study found a simple way to remove a significant number of VOCs within a 24-hour period: add plants to indoor spaces. (5) ___ spending prolonged periods of time indoors, away from plants, deprives people of these benefits.
Which transition word or phrase should be added to the beginning of sentence 5? Move the correct answer to the box.

Drag-the-word format — drag one of these into the box:

  • As a result,
  • Primarily,
  • In contrast,
  • Unfortunately,
Correct: Unfortunately,
Why: Sentence 4 says plants are a known fix; sentence 5 describes people missing out on that fix — that's a disappointing turn, which "Unfortunately" signals (not a cause/effect or example word).
Exam B — "The End of an Era" (BR02)
(4) During the rover's mission, a high-resolution camera on the rover's robotic arm took magnified photographs of the surface of Mars. (5) The photographs revealed small round rocks scattered across the surface of the planet. (6) The scientists nicknamed these rocks "blueberries" based on the rocks' resemblance to the fruit. (7) These rocks were important because their spherical shape suggested that liquid water may have flowed over them for a substantial amount of time. (8) ___ Opportunity continued to provide valuable data about craters and other surface features, traveling a total of 28.06 miles across Mars' surface.
Which word should be added to the beginning of sentence 8? Move the correct answer to the box.

Drag-the-word format — drag one of these into the box:

  • Overall,
  • Furthermore,
  • Meanwhile,
  • Consequently,
Correct: Meanwhile,
Why: The paragraph is shifting to a different, ongoing part of the same mission (surface travel/craters) rather than contrasting it or showing it was caused by the blueberries — "meanwhile" marks that parallel-but-separate shift.
Exam C — "NYC and the Hudson Valley Brickmaking Industry" (CR46)
(10) Builders turned to brick, which was an affordable material that met safety standards while satisfying the city's large demand for buildings. (11) ___ the brickmaking industry in the Hudson Valley skyrocketed.
Which phrase should be added to the beginning of sentence 11 to provide the best transition from the ideas in sentence 10?
  • A. While adapting to new uses for brick,
  • B. Because the building boom preferred brick,
  • C. As a result of New York City's building boom,
  • D. Although reliance on brick became the norm,
Correct: As a result of New York City's building boom,
Why: The city's building boom (sentence 10) is the direct cause of the Hudson Valley's brick industry booming too (sentence 11) — this is a clean cause → effect link, not a contrast ("although") or a side-adaptation ("while adapting to").
Kaplan 2 — "Weaving" (KM2_Q10)
(1) Handweaving, traditionally the province of home spinners and weavers, has been practiced throughout recorded history. (2) ___ silk weaving has been known in China since 3500 B.C.E and in Egypt since 3600 B.C.E.
Which transition should be added to the beginning of sentence 2?
  • E. However,
  • F. Indeed,
  • G. As a result,
  • H. Even so,
Correct: Indeed,
Why: Sentence 2 doesn't contrast or cause sentence 1 — it just backs it up with a concrete, reinforcing example of how old handweaving really is. "Indeed" signals "here's proof," not "however" or "as a result."
2Organization & Sentence Placement3 examples

A sentence is in the wrong spot (or a new sentence needs to be inserted) — the paragraph's ideas need to be reordered so one detail doesn't interrupt a chain of events or separate a claim from the sentence it's supporting.

How to solve it:
  • Read the whole paragraph first and mentally number what order the ideas "should" go in (chronological, general-to-specific, claim-then-evidence).
  • Check whether the sentence in question fits where it currently sits.
  • Or whether it interrupts a pair of sentences that clearly belong next to each other.
Exam D — "Martial Arts for the Mind and Body" (DR02)
Paragraph 2 (sentences 6–10): (6) Discipline, focus, and respect are important qualities for everyone to have. (7) However, for most people these qualities are not innate; they must be learned and practiced. (8) The study of martial arts can provide an opportunity to develop these skills. (9) Students are rewarded for their dedication by passing tests and advancing to higher ranks or levels. (10) For example, in a typical tae kwon do class, students learn discipline by diligently practicing moves, improve focus by listening carefully, and demonstrate respect by bowing to the instructor and following directions.
Where should sentence 10 be moved to improve the organization of the second paragraph?
  • A. to the beginning of the paragraph (before sentence 6)
  • B. between sentences 6 and 7
  • C. between sentences 7 and 8
  • D. between sentences 8 and 9
Move sentence 10 to between sentences 8 and 9.
Why: Sentence 10 is a concrete example of the claim in sentence 8 ("martial arts can provide an opportunity to develop these skills"), so it should sit right after it — currently it's stranded after sentence 9, which has already moved on to a different point (rewards/ranks).
Exam B — "The End of an Era" (BR04)
(4) During the rover's mission, a high-resolution camera on the rover's robotic arm took magnified photographs of the surface of Mars. (5) The photographs revealed small round rocks scattered across the surface of the planet. (6) The scientists nicknamed these rocks "blueberries" based on the rocks' resemblance to the fruit. (7) These rocks were important because their spherical shape suggested that liquid water may have flowed over them for a substantial amount of time.
New sentence: "One of Opportunity's major contributions was finding possible evidence of the presence of water on Mars." Where should this sentence be added to the passage?
  • A. between sentences 2 and 3
  • B. at the beginning of the second paragraph (before sentence 4)
  • C. at the end of the second paragraph (after sentence 7)
  • D. between sentences 8 and 9
Add it at the beginning of the second paragraph (before sentence 4).
Why: It's a topic sentence for the whole "blueberry rocks" paragraph — stating the big-picture finding (evidence of water) before the paragraph explains the specific detail (round rocks) that supports it.
Kaplan 1 — "Nikola Tesla" (KM1_Q12)
(11) His most well-known invention, at least to the general public, was probably the Tesla coil, a power supply with lightning bolt-like fingers of electricity shooting from the base. (12) Indeed, it was this coil that brought the monster to life in the movie version of Frankenstein. (13) He also developed wireless lighting systems and the AC generator. (14) Tesla developed a number of devices, including an induction motor using the then-controversial alternating current.
Where should sentence 14 be moved to improve the organization of the third paragraph?
  • E. to the beginning of the paragraph (before sentence 11)
  • F. between sentences 11 and 12
  • G. between sentences 12 and 13
  • H. at the end of the paragraph (where it is now)
Move sentence 14 to the beginning of the paragraph (before sentence 11).
Why: Sentence 14 is the general umbrella statement ("Tesla developed a number of devices"), and sentences 11–13 are the specific examples under it (the coil, wireless lighting, the AC generator). The general claim belongs before its examples, not stranded after them.
3Off-Topic Removal4 examples

A sentence that's true and well-written, but drifts away from the specific topic of its own paragraph (not the whole passage — just that paragraph) and should be deleted.

How to solve it:
  • State the paragraph's one main point in a single short phrase.
  • Check each sentence against that phrase, not against the passage as a whole.
  • A sentence can be interesting and on-topic for the passage overall but still off-topic for its specific paragraph.
Exam A — "The Benefits of Indoor Plants" (AR46)
(11) Adding plants to indoor spaces has psychological benefits too. (12) Research has long linked time spent in natural environments with increased energy and feelings of contentment. (13) While being outdoors is an excellent option for improving a person's mental health, recent research has indicated that encountering natural elements while indoors can also help. (14) To experience the maximum benefit of natural elements, experts suggest placing at least one live plant per 100 square feet of home or office space. (15) Connecting with nature, even just by being near an indoor plant, is a significant factor in a person's well-being. (16) Sitting in front of an electronic screen all day isn't natural, and today's workers need to get up and get outdoors. (17) Richard Ryan, a psychology professor at the University of Rochester, puts it this way: "Nature is something within which we flourish, so having it be more a part of our lives is critical, especially when we live and work in built environments."
Which sentence presents ideas irrelevant to the topic of the passage and should be deleted?
  • A. sentence 11
  • B. sentence 14
  • C. sentence 15
  • D. sentence 16
(16) Sitting in front of an electronic screen all day isn't natural, and today's workers need to get up and get outdoors.
Why: The whole passage is about bringing plants indoors — sentence 16 instead argues people should go outdoors, which is a different (almost opposite) suggestion from the passage's actual argument.
Exam C — "NYC and the Hudson Valley Brickmaking Industry" (CR45)
(3) Nearly 65 million people visit New York City every year to see these and other attractions. ... (14) Population growth in the Hudson River Valley after the mid-1800s was substantial, though percentage increases were smaller than those in New York City.
Which two sentences present information irrelevant to the ideas in the passage and should be deleted? Select the two correct answers.
  • A. "Nearly 65 million people visit New York City every year to see these and other attractions." (sentence 3)
  • B. "The city's population ballooned from about 313,000 people in 1840 to more than 7 million a century later." (sentence 8)
  • C. "Population growth in the Hudson River Valley after the mid-1800s was substantial, though percentage increases were smaller than those in New York City." (sentence 14)
  • D. "History has proven, however, that boom times eventually end." (sentence 18)
  • E. "Throughout the city, famous and ordinary buildings stand as evidence of the remarkable productivity achieved by the Valley's brickmakers." (sentence 23)
Sentence 3 (tourist visitor count) and sentence 14 (Hudson Valley population growth rate).
Why: The passage is about brickmaking demand and supply, not tourism numbers or general population statistics — both sentences are true and vaguely related to the setting, but neither supports the brick-industry argument.
Kaplan 2 — "States of Consciousness" (KM2_Q08)
(12) Psychologists have found that people are affected by information that they are not aware of consciously. (13) This information is acting on a subconscious level. (14) According to Austrian psychologist Sigmund Freud, people possess subconscious longings that are often socially unacceptable. (15) Two examples that illustrate subconscious processing are priming and the mere-exposure effect. (16) Priming refers to the processing of information from stimuli that are outside of conscious awareness. (17) In fact, studies show that people can correctly answer questions that they have seen previously—even when they do not remember seeing the questions. (18) The mere-exposure effect happens when people prefer stimuli they have seen previously over new stimuli—even when they do not remember the old stimuli.
Which sentence presents ideas irrelevant to the topic of paragraph 4 and should be deleted?
  • E. sentence 14
  • F. sentence 15
  • G. sentence 16
  • H. sentence 17
(14) According to Austrian psychologist Sigmund Freud, people possess subconscious longings that are often socially unacceptable.
Why: The paragraph's actual topic is priming and the mere-exposure effect (two specific research phenomena); Freud's unrelated claim about repressed longings is a different idea about the subconscious that doesn't connect to either.

(15) Mehl-Madrona's focus on ceremony and ritual has influenced many groups. (16) He also improves his mental stamina through the game of chess. (18) Incorporating the ceremonial aspect of the traditional shvitz, participants gather in a willow structure covered with blankets, one of which bears the Star of David. Which sentence presents information that shifts away from the main topic of paragraph 4 and should be removed?
  • E. sentence 15
  • F. sentence 16
  • G. sentence 17
  • H. sentence 18
(16) He also improves his mental stamina through the game of chess.
Why: The paragraph is about sweat-lodge ceremony traditions crossing into Jewish practice — Dr. Mehl-Madrona's chess hobby has nothing to do with that, even though he's a person already mentioned in the paragraph.
4Best Introductory/Concluding/Supporting Sentence5 examples

Pick the sentence that best opens a passage/paragraph, best wraps it up, or best backs up a claim that was just made — without introducing brand-new information that wasn't already building up to it.

How to solve it:
  • Name the paragraph's main claim in your own words first.
  • The right answer restates or reinforces that exact claim at the right altitude — general for an intro, matching the specific topic for a conclusion, a concrete specific example for support.
  • Wrong answers are usually too narrow (introductions), too broad or a new topic (conclusions), or true-but-irrelevant-to-this-specific-claim (support).
Exam A — "The Benefits of Indoor Plants" (AR47, concluding)
(15) Connecting with nature, even just by being near an indoor plant, is a significant factor in a person's well-being. (16) Sitting in front of an electronic screen all day isn't natural, and today's workers need to get up and get outdoors. (Sentence 16 is the one flagged and removed in the Off-Topic Removal category — shown here in full since it's still part of the original passage.) (17) Richard Ryan, a psychology professor at the University of Rochester, puts it this way: "Nature is something within which we flourish, so having it be more a part of our lives is critical, especially when we live and work in built environments."
Which concluding sentence should follow sentence 17 to best support the information presented in the passage?
  • A. Because indoor plants absorb the carbon dioxide in our air and release the oxygen we need to breathe, they are vital to our wholeness and wellness.
  • B. Experts say that adding a Boston fern, a spider plant, or an aloe vera plant is a good place to start if you want to begin to incorporate nature into your home or office.
  • C. More people should consider bringing natural elements inside to improve general wellness and reverse some of the negative effects of an indoor-centered society.
  • D. As one study has confirmed, houseplants are a wise investment because they can remove almost 90 percent of the toxins in the air within the span of 24 hours.
Correct: "More people should consider bringing natural elements inside to improve general wellness and reverse some of the negative effects of an indoor-centered society."
Why: It restates the passage's actual overall argument (bring nature indoors) at the right general level. The wrong choices either over-focus on one narrow detail (just VOC removal, or naming specific plant species) instead of the big-picture takeaway.
Exam C — "NYC and the Hudson Valley Brickmaking Industry" (CR47, supporting)
(18) History has proven, however, that boom times eventually end. (19) Shifting architectural trends halted not only the Hudson Valley's brickmaking boom, but its entire industry. (20) Starting early in the twentieth century, architects and builders increasingly favored steel, glass, and concrete instead of brick. (21) By 1979, reduced demand for brick had driven every Hudson Valley brick factory out of business.
Which sentence would best follow sentence 20 to support ideas in the fourth paragraph?
  • A. At first many people thought the new materials did not look as strong as brick, but eventually the public embraced the trend.
  • B. Increasingly expensive shipping and labor costs also drove up the costs for brickmaking factories.
  • C. The Flatiron building, completed in 1902, is a notable example of an early steel-framed building.
  • D. The more that New York City builders chose to use these materials, the fewer bricks they bought.
Correct: "The more that New York City builders chose to use these materials, the fewer bricks they bought."
Why: It directly connects the architectural shift (sentence 20) to the paragraph's actual topic — the brick industry's decline. The other choices are true historical facts but drift into shipping costs or specific buildings instead of closing the "architects chose other materials → bricks declined" logic.
Exam D — "Martial Arts for the Mind and Body" (DR05, supporting)
Final paragraph: (18) Adults who practice martial arts can experience many of the same benefits that younger people do, but perhaps the greatest of these is health and fitness.
Which sentence would best follow and support sentence 18?
  • A. Adults often enjoy spending time with other students in their martial arts class.
  • B. Adults who study martial arts can apply the discipline they learn to other parts of their lives.
  • C. For example, some adults lose weight and gain muscle tone after only a few months of training.
  • D. In fact, many adults begin studying martial arts because a friend or family member recommended it.
Correct: "For example, some adults lose weight and gain muscle tone after only a few months of training."
Why: Sentence 18's claim is specifically about "health and fitness" — this choice gives a concrete fitness example. The other options support martial arts generally (community, discipline) but not that specific health/fitness claim.
Kaplan 1 — "Nikola Tesla" (KM1_Q14, concluding)
(1) Movies and television shows often portray the scientist as an adherent to the rules—a square, upstanding member of society. (2) Consider the brainy Lisa Simpson in The Simpsons, the geeks in The Big Bang Theory, and even Mr. Spock in the Star Trek series. (3) However, the stupidity of this stereotype is revealed by the life and work of Nikola Tesla, the scientist, inventor, and thinker. Final sentence (19), at the very end of the passage, currently reads: "He was the picture-perfect example of the America[n] Dream."
Which concluding sentence should replace sentence 19 to best support the topic presented in the passage?
  • E. Thanks to his restraint, the Brooklyn Bridge is still standing.
  • F. An intellectual pioneer, Tesla was anything but "square."
  • G. In fact, some modern scientists have invented important devices that help everyone.
  • H. Nevertheless, the stereotype of the rules-bound scientist persists.
Correct: "An intellectual pioneer, Tesla was anything but 'square.'"
Why: It's the only choice that circles back to the passage's actual opening claim (the "square scientist" stereotype) rather than drifting to an unrelated idea ("American Dream") or the wrong supporting detail (the bridge-toppling story, or the stereotype simply persisting).
Kaplan 3 — "Sweat Lodges" (KM3_Q08, introductory)
Sentence 1 currently opens the passage: "Medicines derived from nature have been around for centuries and often complement natural therapies."
Which introductory sentence, if inserted before sentence 1, would best support the argument presented in the passage?
  • E. As people become more interested in healthy living, many new trends have emerged.
  • F. The twenty-first century has seen a marked increase in the popularity of natural medicines and therapies.
  • G. Natural remedies and approaches are an integral part of living the healthiest way possible.
  • H. Some people may be skeptical about natural approaches to treating ailments, but many people find them helpful.
Correct: "The twenty-first century has seen a marked increase in the popularity of natural medicines and therapies."
Why: It's a broad, factual framing statement that smoothly leads into sentence 1's topic (natural medicines/therapies) without taking a side ("skeptical," "integral part of healthiest living") the passage itself never actually argues.
5Style & Tone Matching4 examples

A sentence needs to be rewritten in different words while keeping the SAME formal, objective style as the rest of the passage — no slang, no casual asides, no needless flourishes, no second-guessing the facts.

How to solve it:
  • Ignore grammar correctness for a second — every answer choice is usually grammatically fine.
  • Cross out any choice with casual phrasing ("way too," "you may know"), unnecessary flair/exaggeration, or added emotional commentary that the rest of the passage doesn't use.
  • The correct choice sounds the most like a textbook/encyclopedia, matter-of-fact.
Exam B — "The End of an Era" (BR03)
Sentence 11 currently reads: "However, in 2018, a dust storm spread all over the planet, putting the rover's progress on hold."
Which revision of sentence 11 best maintains the formal style established in the passage?
  • A. However, in 2018, a dust storm covered the planet, bringing the rover's progress to a standstill.
  • B. However, in 2018, a dust storm shrouded the planet, impermanently halting the rover's progress.
  • C. However, in 2018, a dust storm enveloped the whole of the planet, arresting the rover's progress for a time.
  • D. However, in 2018, a dust storm covered up the whole planet, which quickly brought a stop to the progress of the rover.
Correct: "However, in 2018, a dust storm covered the planet, bringing the rover's progress to a standstill."
Why: It's a plain, matter-of-fact restatement. Wrong choices reach for showy vocabulary ("enveloped the whole of the planet, arresting the rover's progress for a time") that sounds more like creative writing than the rest of the passage's plain NASA-report tone.
Exam C — "NYC and the Hudson Valley Brickmaking Industry" (CR44)
Sentence 10 currently reads: "Bricks turned out to be an inexpensive way for builders to quickly throw together a lot of structures while keeping building owners and safety inspectors happy."
Which revision of sentence 10 would best maintain the style established in the passage?
  • A. Builders turned to brick, which was an affordable material that met safety standards while satisfying the city's large demand for buildings.
  • B. Figuring out that bricks were inexpensive, builders used tons of them to satisfy inspectors and the city's desperate construction needs.
  • C. Builders favored brick as a pleasingly economical way to meet significant construction demand while following municipal regulations.
  • D. Once builders realized that low-cost brick was a law-abiding way to meet the city's building needs, they hardly ever used any other materials.
Correct: "Builders turned to brick, which was an affordable material that met safety standards while satisfying the city's large demand for buildings."
Why: It keeps the passage's neutral, informative register. Wrong choices either stay casual ("figuring out that bricks were inexpensive," "tons of them") or add an opinionated exaggeration ("they hardly ever used any other materials") not supported by the passage.
Exam D — "Martial Arts for the Mind and Body" (DR03)
Sentence 12 currently reads: "A lot of teens go through hard situations as they try to do well in school and in life."
Which revision of sentence 12 best maintains the formal style established in the passage?
  • A. A lot of teens have to put up with difficult things while trying to do well in school and in life.
  • B. Many teenagers deal with tough situations as they try to stay on top of their studies and do well in life.
  • C. Many teenagers encounter challenges as they work to succeed both academically and personally.
  • D. A lot of teens face problems as they work to keep up with their schoolwork and find personal success.
Correct: "Many teenagers encounter challenges as they work to succeed both academically and personally."
Why: "A lot of" and "hard situations" are casual phrasing — the correct choice swaps in the more formal "many" and "challenges." The other wrong choices keep casual phrasing like "put up with" or "keep up with."
Kaplan 1 — "Nikola Tesla" (KM1_Q09)
Sentence 3 currently reads: "However, the stupidity of this stereotype is revealed by the life and work of Nikola Tesla, the scientist, inventor, and thinker."
Which revision of sentence 3 best maintains the formal style established in the passage?
  • A. The stupidity of this stereotype is revealed by the life and work of Nikola Tesla.
  • B. Nonetheless, this stereotype falls way too short, as revealed by the life and work of Nikola Tesla, the scientist, inventor, and thinker.
  • C. However, the inadequacy of this stereotype is revealed by the life and work of Nikola Tesla, the scientist, inventor, and thinker.
  • D. As you may know, the weakness of this stereotype is unveiled by the work of Nikola Tesla, the scientist, inventor, and thinker.
Correct: "However, the inadequacy of this stereotype is revealed by the life and work of Nikola Tesla, the scientist, inventor, and thinker."
Why: It swaps the blunt, informal word "stupidity" for the more formal "inadequacy" while changing nothing else. Wrong choices add casual asides ("As you may know...") or drop supporting detail entirely — the goal is a like-for-like word swap, not a rewrite.

Strategy, top to bottom: read the whole numbered paragraph once for meaning before answering. Identify which of the 5 skills above is being tested from the question phrasing. Then check only for that — don't go hunting for unrelated problems. For the short standalone questions, see the Grammar Guide. This guide grows as more material gets transcribed.
